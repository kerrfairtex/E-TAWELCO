      Bongao · Tawi-Tawi · BARMM

      Rooted in the places
      we call home.

      Bongao is a municipality and the provincial capital of Tawi-Tawi in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao. The municipality includes Bongao, Sanga-Sanga and Pababag islands, and part of the Tawi-Tawi mainland—not just the town center.

      Municipal boundaries, physical islands and electricity service areas are different. A barangay address does not establish feeder coverage, connection eligibility or current power status.

      See sources and census dates

      Many communities. Shared island life.

      Tausug, Sama, Sama-Bajau, Christian and other communities are part of Bongao's local identity. English and Filipino interface options are available; Bahasa Sūg and Sinama need fluent local translation and community review.

      Energy for home and livelihood

      Fishing, seaweed-related livelihoods, farming and trade shape everyday life. Household lighting and phone charging are different needs from shop refrigeration or equipment use; the sample estimator is not a business tariff.

      Places with their own stories

      Bud Bongao, the Balobok Rock Shelter in Lakit Lakit and the mangrove eco-park in Lapid Lapid offer local context. Site photographs remain illustrative stock images—not verified images of these places or local households.

      How to read your bill

      The cost of making the electricity at your island's plant, mostly fuel for diesel and upkeep for solar.

      A share of the power that is lost in wires and meters on the way to homes.

      The cost of the poles, lines, meters and people who bring power to your house.

      Government taxes and required charges that the cooperative collects and passes on.

      Get connected

      Bring these to your nearest cooperative office. Steps and documents are sample content until the cooperative confirms them.

      Steps (sample)

      1. Attend the pre-membership seminar at the office or with your island collector.
      2. Fill in the membership and connection application form.
      3. Have a licensed electrician wire your house and get it inspected.
      4. Pay the membership fee and meter deposit.
      5. Wait for the line crew to install your meter, usually within 2 weeks.

      Required documents (sample)

      • One valid ID
      • Proof of house ownership or a letter from the owner
      • Barangay certificate
      • Electrical wiring inspection certificate
      • Two 1x1 photos
